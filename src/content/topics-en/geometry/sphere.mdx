<Definition>A **sphere** is the set of all points of space at distance $R$ from a given point (the centre). A **ball** is the solid bounded by a sphere.</Definition>

<Formulas>
<Formula id="section-formula" name="Radius of a section" tex="\rho = \sqrt{R^2 - d^2}" live="ρ" proof="section-radius" />
<Formula id="volume-formula" name="Volume of a ball" tex="V = \tfrac43\,\pi R^3" live="V" proof="sphere-volume" />
<Formula id="area-formula" name="Area of a sphere" tex="S = 4\pi R^2" proof="sphere-area" />
</Formulas>

<Properties>

<Property id="section-radius" title="A plane section of a ball is a disc whose centre is the foot of the perpendicular from the centre of the ball to the plane, and whose radius is $\sqrt{R^2 - d^2}$" why="Every point of the section is at distance $R$ from the centre, so all are equally far from the foot of the perpendicular.">
<Proof given="A ball with centre $O$, a plane at distance $d$ from $O$, $OP$ the perpendicular" prove="The points of the section are at distance $\sqrt{R^2 - d^2}$ from $P$">
<Step show="OP P" hl="OP">$OP \perp$ the plane, $OP = d$.</Step>
<Step show="PK OK K <OPK" hl="OPK" tex="PK^2 = OK^2 - OP^2 = R^2 - d^2">For any point $K$ of the section: $\triangle OPK$ is right, $OK = R$.</Step>
<Step tex="PK = \sqrt{R^2 - d^2} \quad \blacksquare">The distance does not depend on $K$: the section is a disc bounded by a circle with centre $P$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="sphere-volume" title="The volume of a ball is $\tfrac43\pi R^3$" why="At every height the section of a half-ball equals the difference of the sections of a cylinder and a cone (Archimedes).">
<Proof given="A ball of radius $R$" prove="$V = \tfrac43 \pi R^3$">
<Step tex="\pi(R^2 - z^2) = \pi R^2 - \pi z^2">At height $z$ from the centre the area of the section of the ball is $\pi(R^2 - z^2)$ (previous property).</Step>
<Step>Take a cylinder with radius and height $R$, with a cone cut out of it that has the same base and its apex at the centre of the lower base. At height $z$ its section is a ring of area $\pi R^2 - \pi z^2$, exactly equal to the section of the half-ball.</Step>
<Step tex="V_{1/2} = \pi R^2 \cdot R - \tfrac13\,\pi R^2 \cdot R = \tfrac23\,\pi R^3">By Cavalieri's principle the volumes are equal too.</Step>
<Step tex="V = \tfrac43\,\pi R^3 \quad \blacksquare">Two half-balls.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="sphere-area" title="The area of a sphere is $4\pi R^2$" why="The ball splits into thin pyramids with apex at the centre and height $R$.">
<Proof given="A sphere of radius $R$" prove="$S = 4\pi R^2$">
<Step>Divide the sphere into small pieces and join each to the centre: the ball becomes a sum of pyramids whose height in the limit is $R$, and the sum of the areas of their bases is $S$.</Step>
<Step tex="V = \tfrac13\, S\, R">The sum of the volumes of the pyramids.</Step>
<Step tex="S = \frac{3V}{R} = \frac{3}{R} \cdot \tfrac43\,\pi R^3 = 4\pi R^2 \quad \blacksquare">By the previous property $V = \tfrac43 \pi R^3$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark>A plane that touches a sphere at one point is a **tangent plane**; it is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency (the radius of the section is $\rho = 0$ when $d = R$).</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">A "sphere" is a surface (area $4\pi R^2$), a "ball" is a solid (volume $\tfrac43 \pi R^3$).</Remark>
