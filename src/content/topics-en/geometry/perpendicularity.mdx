<Definition>A line is perpendicular to a plane if it is perpendicular to every line of the plane. If $AH \perp \alpha$, $H \in \alpha$, then $AH$ is the **perpendicular**, any other segment $AB$ ($B \in \alpha$) is an **oblique**, and $HB$ is the **projection** of the oblique. The distance from a point to a plane is the length of the perpendicular.</Definition>

<Properties>

<Property id="line-plane-perpendicular" kind="criterion" title="If a line is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of a plane, it is perpendicular to the plane" why="A chain of congruent triangles makes any line of the plane a perpendicular bisector.">
<Proof given="$AH \perp b$, $AH \perp c$, $b = HE$ and $c = HF$ lines of $\alpha$" prove="$AH \perp x$ for any line $x$ of $\alpha$">
<Step show="HE HF E F" hl="AH HE HF">$AH$ is perpendicular to two lines of $\alpha$, $b = HE$ and $c = HF$.</Step>
<Step show="A'H A' AE A'E AF A'F" hl="AE=A'E AF=A'F">On the extension of $AH$ mark off $HA' = AH$. $b$ and $c$ are perpendicular bisectors of $AA'$, so $AE = A'E$, $AF = A'F$.</Step>
<Step show="EF HX X" hl="AEF A'EF">Take any line $x$ of $\alpha$ through $H$ and a line $EF$ that cuts it at $X$. $\triangle AEF = \triangle A'EF$ (SSS).</Step>
<Step show="AX A'X" hl="AEX A'EX">So $\angle AEX = \angle A'EX$, and $\triangle AEX = \triangle A'EX$ (SAS): $AX = A'X$.</Step>
<Step hl="AXA' HX">$\triangle AXA'$ is isosceles and $XH$ is its median, hence also a height: $AH \perp x$. $x$ was arbitrary, so $AH \perp \alpha$. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="three-perpendiculars" title="The theorem of three perpendiculars: a line of the plane perpendicular to the projection of an oblique is perpendicular to the oblique itself" why="This line turns out to be perpendicular to the plane of the perpendicular and the projection.">
<Proof given="$AH \perp \alpha$, $AB$ an oblique, $c = C_1C_2 \subset \alpha$, $B \in c$, $c \perp HB$" prove="$c \perp AB$">
<Step show="AB HB B C1C2 C1 C2" hl="C1C2 HB">$c$ passes through $B$ and is perpendicular to the projection $HB$.</Step>
<Step hl="C1C2 AH">$AH \perp \alpha$, so $AH \perp c$.</Step>
<Step hl="AHB">$c$ is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of the plane $AHB$, $HB$ and $AH$: by the previous criterion $c \perp (AHB)$.</Step>
<Step hl="C1C2 AB" tex="c \perp AB \quad \blacksquare">$AB$ lies in this plane.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="three-perpendiculars-converse" kind="converse" title="The converse: a line of the plane perpendicular to an oblique is perpendicular to its projection as well" why="The same argument: the line is perpendicular to $AB$ and to $AH$.">
<Proof given="$AH \perp \alpha$, $c \subset \alpha$, $B \in c$, $c \perp AB$" prove="$c \perp HB$">
<Step show="AB HB B C1C2 C1 C2" hl="C1C2 AB">$c \perp AB$ and $c \perp AH$ ($AH$ is perpendicular to the whole of $\alpha$).</Step>
<Step hl="AHB" tex="c \perp (AHB) \;\Rightarrow\; c \perp HB \quad \blacksquare">$c$ is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of the plane $AHB$, and $HB$ lies in this plane.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="perpendicular-shortest-3d" title="The perpendicular from a point to a plane is shorter than any oblique" why="The oblique is the hypotenuse of a right triangle.">
<Proof given="$AH \perp \alpha$, $AB$ an oblique" prove="$AB > AH$">
<Step show="AB HB B" hl="AHB" tex="AB^2 = AH^2 + HB^2">$AH \perp HB$: $\triangle AHB$ is right.</Step>
<Step tex="AB > AH \quad \blacksquare">$HB > 0$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark>Of two obliques drawn from one point, the one with the longer projection is longer; equal obliques have equal projections.</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">For a line to be perpendicular to a plane, being perpendicular to one line is not enough: two intersecting lines are needed.</Remark>

<Problem id="p-perp-three" set={{ h: 2.6, t: 0.4, th: -25 }} show="AB HB C1C2 C1 C2" prove="<HBC1=90 <ABC1=90" book="14.78"
  q="$AH$ is perpendicular to a plane, the line $C_1C_2$ lies in this plane and $HB \perp C_1C_2$. Prove that $AB \perp C_1C_2$."
  hint="$C_1C_2$ is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of the plane $AHB$.">
<Step hl="AH C1C2" tex="AH \perp C_1C_2">$AH$ is perpendicular to the plane, so it is perpendicular to every line lying in it.</Step>
<Step hl="HB C1C2" tex="HB \perp C_1C_2">Given.</Step>
<Step hl="AHB" tex="C_1C_2 \perp (AHB)">$C_1C_2$ is perpendicular to two intersecting lines of the plane $AHB$, $AH$ and $HB$.</Step>
<Step hl="AB C1C2" tex="AB \perp C_1C_2 \quad \blacksquare">$AB$ lies in this plane: this is the theorem of three perpendiculars.</Step>
</Problem>
