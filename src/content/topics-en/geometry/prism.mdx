<Definition>A **prism** is a polyhedron two of whose faces (the bases) are congruent polygons in parallel planes, while the others (the lateral faces) are parallelograms. A prism is right if its lateral edges are perpendicular to the base. A **parallelepiped** is a prism whose base is a parallelogram; all faces of a **cuboid** (rectangular parallelepiped) are rectangles.</Definition>

<Properties>

<Property id="parallelepiped-diagonals" title="All four diagonals of a parallelepiped meet at one point and are bisected by it" why="Every two diagonals are the diagonals of one parallelogram.">
<Proof given="$ABCDA_1B_1C_1D_1$ a parallelepiped" prove="$AC_1, BD_1, CA_1, DB_1$ meet at $O$ and are bisected">
<Step show="AC1 CA1 AC A1C1" hl="ACC1A1">$AA_1$ and $CC_1$ are equal and parallel (both to $BB_1$), so $ACC_1A_1$ is a parallelogram.</Step>
<Step hl="AC1 CA1">The diagonals $AC_1$ and $CA_1$ of a parallelogram bisect each other.</Step>
<Step show="BD1 AD1 BC1" hl="ABC1D1">$AB$ and $D_1C_1$ are equal and parallel, so $ABC_1D_1$ is a parallelogram too: $AC_1$ and $BD_1$ have a common midpoint.</Step>
<Step show="DB1 O" hl="AC1 BD1 CA1 DB1">Likewise for $DB_1$. All four diagonals have the common midpoint $O$. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="box-diagonal" title="The square of the diagonal of a cuboid is the sum of the squares of its three dimensions" why="Pythagoras' theorem twice: in the base and in the diagonal section.">
<Proof given="A cuboid, $AB = a$, $BC = b$, $CC_1 = c$" prove="$AC_1^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2$">
<Step set={{ tilt: 0 }} show="AC" hl="ABC" tex="AC^2 = a^2 + b^2">The right triangle $\triangle ABC$ in the base.</Step>
<Step show="AC1" hl="ACC1" tex="AC_1^2 = AC^2 + CC_1^2">$CC_1$ is perpendicular to the base, so $CC_1 \perp AC$: $\triangle ACC_1$ is right.</Step>
<Step tex="d^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 \quad \blacksquare">Substitute.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="prism-lateral" title="The lateral surface of a right prism is the perimeter of the base times the height" why="The lateral faces are rectangles of the same height.">
<Proof given="A right prism, base perimeter $P$, height $h$" prove="$S_{\text{lat}} = P\,h$">
<Step set={{ tilt: 0 }} hl="ABB1A1 BCC1B1 CDD1C1 DAA1D1">In a right prism the lateral faces are rectangles, all with height $h$.</Step>
<Step tex="\begin{aligned}S_{\text{lat}} &= AB\cdot h + BC\cdot h + CD\cdot h + DA\cdot h \\ &= P\,h \quad \blacksquare\end{aligned}">The sum of the areas of the rectangles.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="prism-volume" title="The volume of a prism is the area of the base times the height" why="An oblique prism has the same cross-section at every height as a right one.">
<Proof given="A prism, base area $S$, height $h$" prove="$V = S\,h$">
<Step set={{ tilt: 0 }} tex="V = abc = S\,h">The volume of a cuboid is $abc$: it holds $a \cdot b \cdot c$ unit cubes.</Step>
<Step set={{ tilt: 25 }} show="A1H H" hl="A1H">In an oblique prism the height is $h = A_1H$, the distance between the bases.</Step>
<Step tex="V = S\,h \quad \blacksquare">Every cross-section parallel to the base is congruent to the base, just as in a right prism with the same base and height. By Cavalieri's principle, solids whose cross-sections at every height have equal areas have equal volumes.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark kind="special">A cube is a cuboid with equal edges: $d = a\sqrt3$, $V = a^3$, $S_{\text{tot}} = 6a^2$.</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">The height of an oblique prism is not its lateral edge: the height is perpendicular to the bases, the edge is oblique.</Remark>
