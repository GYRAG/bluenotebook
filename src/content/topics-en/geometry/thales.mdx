<Definition>Segments $a, b$ and $c, d$ are **proportional** if $\tfrac{a}{b} = \tfrac{c}{d}$.</Definition>

<Properties>

<Property id="thales" title="Thales' theorem: if equal segments are marked off on one side of an angle and parallel lines are drawn through their ends, the lines cut equal segments on the other side too" why="Two parallelograms carry the equality to the other side; congruent triangles do the rest.">
<Proof given="$A_1A_2 = A_2A_3$, $A_1B_1 \parallel A_2B_2 \parallel A_3B_3$" prove="$B_1B_2 = B_2B_3$">
<Step set={{ m: 2, n: 2 }} hl="A1A2=A2A3 A1B1||A2B2||A3B3">$A_1A_2 = A_2A_3$, and the lines $A_1B_1$, $A_2B_2$, $A_3B_3$ are parallel.</Step>
<Step show="B1C2 B2C3 C2 C3" hl="B1C2 B2C3">Through $B_1$ and $B_2$ draw lines parallel to $OA_3$: $B_1C_2 \parallel A_1A_2$, $B_2C_3 \parallel A_2A_3$.</Step>
<Step hl="A1A2C2B1 A2A3C3B2" tex="B_1C_2 = A_1A_2 = A_2A_3 = B_2C_3">$A_1A_2C_2B_1$ and $A_2A_3C_3B_2$ are parallelograms, so their opposite sides are equal.</Step>
<Step hl="B1C2B2 B2C3B3" tex="\triangle B_1C_2B_2 = \triangle B_2C_3B_3">$\angle C_2B_1B_2 = \angle C_3B_2B_3$ and $\angle B_1C_2B_2 = \angle B_2C_3B_3$, since their sides are parallel in pairs. ASA.</Step>
<Step hl="B1B2=B2B3" tex="B_1B_2 = B_2B_3 \quad \blacksquare">Corresponding sides.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="proportional" title="Parallel lines crossing the sides of an angle cut proportional segments on them" why="A parallel line cuts off a similar triangle.">
<Proof given="$A_1B_1 \parallel A_2B_2$" prove="$\tfrac{OA_1}{A_1A_2} = \tfrac{OB_1}{B_1B_2}$">
<Step hl="A1B1||A2B2">$A_1B_1 \parallel A_2B_2$.</Step>
<Step hl="OA1B1 OA2B2" tex="\frac{OA_1}{OA_2} = \frac{OB_1}{OB_2}">$\triangle OA_1B_1 \sim \triangle OA_2B_2$: a line parallel to a side cuts off a similar triangle.</Step>
<Step tex="\frac{OA_1}{A_1A_2} = \frac{OB_1}{B_1B_2}">Flip the ratios and subtract $1$ from both: $OA_2 - OA_1 = A_1A_2$, $OB_2 - OB_1 = B_1B_2$.</Step>
<Step hl="A1A2 A2A3 B1B2 B2B3" tex="\frac{A_1A_2}{A_2A_3} = \frac{B_1B_2}{B_2B_3} \quad \blacksquare">The same argument for $A_2B_2 \parallel A_3B_3$, then divide the equalities.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark kind="special">The midline property of a triangle follows from Thales' theorem: a line through the midpoint of one side parallel to another side bisects the third side.</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">The theorem compares segments on the same side of the angle. $\tfrac{A_1B_1}{A_2B_2}$ (the parallel segments themselves) equals $\tfrac{OA_1}{OA_2}$, not $\tfrac{A_1A_2}{A_2A_3}$.</Remark>
