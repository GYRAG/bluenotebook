<Definition>The **angle between a line and a plane** is the angle between the line and its projection. A **dihedral angle** is a figure made of two half-planes bounded by one line (the edge). Its **linear angle** is the angle formed by the rays drawn from one point of the edge, in both half-planes, perpendicular to the edge. Planes are perpendicular if the dihedral angle between them is right.</Definition>

<Properties>

<Property id="linear-angle-equal" title="All linear angles of a dihedral angle are equal" why="A translation along the edge lays one linear angle onto another.">
<Proof given="$\angle TKT'$ and $\angle M_2M_1M_3$ linear angles" prove="$\angle TKT' = \angle M_2M_1M_3$">
<Step show="KT KT' K M1M2 M1M3 M1 M2 M3" hl="<TKT' <M2M1M3">The sides of both angles are perpendicular to the edge and lie in the corresponding half-planes.</Step>
<Step hl="KT M1M2">$KT \parallel M_1M_2$: in one plane, lines perpendicular to one line are parallel; likewise $KT' \parallel M_1M_3$.</Step>
<Step>The translation by $\overrightarrow{KM_1}$ takes $K$ to $M_1$ and the rays to the corresponding parallel rays: one angle lies on the other. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="projection-area" title="The area of the orthogonal projection of a polygon equals its area times the cosine of the angle between the planes" why="The height, perpendicular to the edge, is multiplied by $\cos\varphi$, and the base does not change.">
<Proof given="$\triangle E_1E_2T$, $E_1E_2$ on the edge, $T'$ the projection of $T$" prove="$S' = S\cos\varphi$">
<Step show="E1E2T E1T' E2T' TT' T' KT KT' K" hl="<TKT'">$TK \perp E_1E_2$ (the height), and by the converse of the theorem of three perpendiculars $T'K \perp E_1E_2$. So $\angle TKT' = \varphi$ is a linear angle.</Step>
<Step hl="TKT'" tex="T'K = TK\cos\varphi">The right triangle $\triangle TT'K$.</Step>
<Step hl="E1E2T'" tex="S' = \tfrac12\,E_1E_2 \cdot TK\cos\varphi = S\cos\varphi \quad \blacksquare">The base is the same, the height is multiplied by $\cos\varphi$. Any polygon can be cut by lines parallel to the edge into such triangles (and their differences).</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="planes-perpendicular" kind="criterion" title="If a plane passes through a line perpendicular to another plane, the planes are perpendicular" why="This line itself forms a linear angle, a right one.">
<Proof given="$KT \perp$ the lower plane, $KT \subset$ the upper plane" prove="The planes are perpendicular">
<Step set={{ phi: 90 }} show="KT K" hl="KT">$KT$ is perpendicular to the lower plane and lies in the upper plane, and $K$ is on the edge.</Step>
<Step show="KT'" hl="<TKT'">In the lower plane draw $KT' \perp E_1E_2$. $KT$ is perpendicular to the whole lower plane, so $KT \perp KT'$: the linear angle is right. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark kind="mistake">A dihedral angle is measured between rays perpendicular to the edge. Any two other rays in the two faces (not perpendicular to the edge) give a different angle.</Remark>
