<Definition>A trapezoid is a quadrilateral with two parallel opposite sides while the other two are not parallel. The parallel sides ($AD \parallel BC$) are the **bases**, the other two are the **legs**. The segment joining the midpoints of the legs is the **midline** of the trapezoid.</Definition>

<Remark kind="convention">In Georgian schools a trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides, so a parallelogram is not a trapezoid. Some textbooks (especially in English) count every quadrilateral with *at least* one pair of parallel sides as a trapezoid; then a parallelogram is a trapezoid too.</Remark>

<Properties>

<Property id="angles-on-legs" title="The angles at one leg add up to $180^\circ$" why="These are co-interior angles at the parallel bases.">
<Proof given="$ABCD$ a trapezoid, $AD \parallel BC$" prove="$\angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ,\ \angle C + \angle D = 180^\circ$">
<Step hl="AD||BC AB">$AD \parallel BC$, and the leg $AB$ is a transversal.</Step>
<Step hl="<A <B" tex="\angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ">$\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are co-interior angles.</Step>
<Step hl="<C <D" tex="\angle C + \angle D = 180^\circ \quad \blacksquare">The same for the other leg (transversal $CD$).</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="midline" title="The midline is parallel to the bases and equal to half their sum" why="The trapezoid turns into a triangle with the same midline.">
<Proof given="$M, N$ the midpoints of the legs $AB$ and $CD$" prove="$MN \parallel AD,\ MN = \tfrac{a + b}{2}$">
<Step show="M N" hl="M N">$M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of the legs.</Step>
<Step show="BE DE E" hl="BE">Draw $BN$ and extend it until it meets line $AD$ at $E$.</Step>
<Step hl="BCN EDN CN=DN" tex="\triangle BCN = \triangle EDN">$CN = DN$, $\angle BNC = \angle END$ (vertical), $\angle BCN = \angle EDN$ (alternate, $BC \parallel AE$): the triangles are congruent (ASA).</Step>
<Step hl="BN=NE BC=DE" tex="BN = NE, \qquad DE = BC = b">Corresponding sides of congruent triangles.</Step>
<Step show="MN" hl="MN ABE" tex="MN = \tfrac12 AE = \tfrac{a + b}{2} \quad \blacksquare">$MN$ joins the midpoints of the sides $AB$ and $BE$ of $\triangle ABE$, so it is a midline of that triangle; $AE = AD + DE = a + b$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="area" title="The area of a trapezoid is half the sum of the bases times the height" why="A diagonal cuts it into two triangles with the same height.">
<Proof given="$AD = a,\ BC = b$, height $h$" prove="$S = \tfrac{a + b}{2}\, h$">
<Step show="BD BH <BHD" hl="BD">Draw the diagonal $BD$ and the height $BH$.</Step>
<Step hl="ABD" tex="S_{ABD} = \tfrac12\, a\,h">The base of $\triangle ABD$ is $AD = a$, its height $h$.</Step>
<Step hl="BCD" tex="S_{BCD} = \tfrac12\, b\,h">The base of $\triangle BCD$ is $BC = b$; the distance from $D$ to line $BC$ is also $h$ (the distance between parallel lines).</Step>
<Step tex="S = \tfrac12 a h + \tfrac12 b h = \frac{a + b}{2}\, h = m\,h \quad \blacksquare">Add them up.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="diagonals-ratio" title="The point where the diagonals meet divides each diagonal in the ratio of the bases" why="The two triangles on the bases are similar.">
<Proof given="$O = AC \cap BD$" prove="$AO : OC = DO : OB = a : b$">
<Step show="AC BD O" hl="AC BD">Draw the diagonals.</Step>
<Step hl="<AOD=<COB <OAD=<OCB">$\angle AOD = \angle COB$ (vertical), $\angle OAD = \angle OCB$ (alternate, $AD \parallel BC$).</Step>
<Step hl="AOD BOC" tex="\begin{gathered}\triangle AOD \sim \triangle COB \\ \frac{AO}{OC} = \frac{DO}{OB} = \frac{AD}{CB} = \frac{a}{b} \quad \blacksquare\end{gathered}">The triangles are similar by two angles.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="segment-through-o" title="The segment through the intersection of the diagonals, parallel to the bases, is $\tfrac{2ab}{a+b}$, and $O$ bisects it" why="Two pairs of similar triangles give the same $\tfrac{ab}{a+b}$.">
<Proof given="$PQ \parallel AD$, $O \in PQ$, $P \in AB$, $Q \in CD$" prove="$PQ = \tfrac{2ab}{a + b}$">
<Step show="AC BD O PQ P Q" hl="PQ" tex="\frac{AO}{AC} = \frac{a}{a + b}">From the previous property $AO : OC = a : b$.</Step>
<Step hl="APO ABC" tex="\frac{PO}{BC} = \frac{AO}{AC} \;\Rightarrow\; PO = \frac{ab}{a + b}">$PO \parallel BC$, so $\triangle APO \sim \triangle ABC$.</Step>
<Step hl="DQO DCB" tex="\frac{OQ}{BC} = \frac{DO}{DB} = \frac{a}{a + b} \;\Rightarrow\; OQ = \frac{ab}{a + b}">$OQ \parallel BC$, so $\triangle DQO \sim \triangle DCB$.</Step>
<Step hl="PO=OQ" tex="PQ = PO + OQ = \frac{2ab}{a + b} \quad \blacksquare">$PO = OQ$: $O$ is the midpoint of this segment.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="diagonal-midpoints" title="The midpoints of the diagonals lie on the midline, and the distance between them is half the difference of the bases ($a > b$)" why="Two triangle midlines pass through $M$: $\tfrac{b}{2}$ and $\tfrac{a}{2}$.">
<Proof given="$X, Y$ the midpoints of the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$, $a > b$" prove="$X, Y \in MN,\ XY = \tfrac{a - b}{2}$">
<Step show="AC BD X Y M" hl="X Y">$X$ and $Y$ are the midpoints of the diagonals, $M$ the midpoint of the leg $AB$.</Step>
<Step show="MX" hl="MX ABC" tex="MX \parallel BC, \qquad MX = \tfrac{b}{2}">$MX$ joins the midpoints of two sides of $\triangle ABC$, so it is a midline of that triangle.</Step>
<Step show="MY" hl="MY ABD" tex="MY \parallel AD, \qquad MY = \tfrac{a}{2}">$MY$ is a midline of $\triangle ABD$.</Step>
<Step show="MN N" hl="MN">$MX$ and $MY$ pass through $M$ and are parallel to the bases. Only one line through $M$ is parallel to the bases, the line of the midline, so $X$ and $Y$ lie on it.</Step>
<Step hl="XY" tex="XY = MY - MX = \frac{a - b}{2} \quad \blacksquare">$X$ and $Y$ are on the same side of $M$, and $MX < MY$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="diagonal-triangles" title="The diagonals divide a trapezoid into four triangles: the triangles at the legs have equal areas, and the square of each area is the product of the areas of the triangles at the bases" why="Triangles with the same base and height have equal areas.">
<Proof given="$O = AC \cap BD$" prove="$S_{AOB} = S_{COD},\ S_{AOB}^2 = S_{AOD} \cdot S_{BOC}$">
<Step show="AC BD O" hl="ABD ACD" tex="S_{ABD} = S_{ACD}">The two triangles share the base $AD$, and their heights are equal: $B$ and $C$ are equally far from $AD$.</Step>
<Step hl="AOB COD" tex="\begin{aligned}S_{AOB} &= S_{ABD} - S_{AOD} \\ &= S_{ACD} - S_{AOD} = S_{COD}\end{aligned}">Subtract $S_{AOD}$ from both.</Step>
<Step hl="AOB AOD" tex="\frac{S_{AOB}}{S_{AOD}} = \frac{OB}{OD}">The triangles share the vertex $A$, and their bases $OB$ and $OD$ lie on one line, so the areas are proportional to the bases.</Step>
<Step hl="BOC COD" tex="\frac{S_{BOC}}{S_{COD}} = \frac{OB}{OD}">The same with the common vertex $C$.</Step>
<Step tex="\begin{gathered}S_{AOB} \cdot S_{COD} = S_{AOD} \cdot S_{BOC} \\ S_{AOB}^2 = S_{AOD} \cdot S_{BOC} \quad \blacksquare\end{gathered}">The two ratios are equal; substitute $S_{COD} = S_{AOB}$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="leg-bisectors" title="The bisectors of the angles at one leg meet at a right angle, and the meeting point lies on the line of the midline" why="The halves add up to $90^\circ$; the median to the hypotenuse is half of it.">
<Proof given="$AL$, $BL$ the bisectors of $\angle A$ and $\angle B$" prove="$\angle ALB = 90^\circ$, $L \in MN$">
<Step show="AL BL L" hl="AL BL">$AL$ and $BL$ bisect the angles at the leg $AB$.</Step>
<Step hl="<ALB" tex="\angle LAB + \angle LBA = \tfrac12(\angle A + \angle B) = 90^\circ">The angles at one leg add up to $180^\circ$, so in $\triangle ALB$, $\angle ALB = 90^\circ$.</Step>
<Step show="ML M" hl="ML=AM" tex="ML = \tfrac12 AB = AM">$LM$ is the median to the hypotenuse of the right triangle $ALB$.</Step>
<Step hl="<MLA=<LAD" tex="\angle MLA = \angle MAL = \angle LAD">$\triangle AML$ is isosceles, and $AL$ is a bisector.</Step>
<Step show="MN N" hl="ML||AD">$\angle MLA$ and $\angle LAD$ are alternate angles, so $ML \parallel AD$. Only the line of the midline passes through $M$ parallel to $AD$, so $L$ lies on it. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark>$\tfrac{2ab}{a+b}$ is the harmonic mean of $a$ and $b$. It is always less than the midline ($\tfrac{a+b}{2}$) when $a \ne b$.</Remark>

<Properties title="Isosceles trapezoid (equal legs)">

<Property id="isosceles-base-angles" title="The angles of an isosceles trapezoid at each base are equal" why="Two heights cut off two congruent right triangles.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD$" prove="$\angle A = \angle D,\ \angle B = \angle C$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} show="BH CK <BHA <CKD" hl="BH CK">Drop the heights $BH$ and $CK$; $BH = CK$ (the distance between the parallel bases).</Step>
<Step hl="ABH DCK AB=CD BH=CK" tex="\triangle ABH = \triangle DCK">The right triangles are congruent by the hypotenuse ($AB = DC$) and a leg ($BH = CK$).</Step>
<Step hl="<A=<D" tex="\angle A = \angle D">Corresponding angles are equal.</Step>
<Step hl="<B=<C" tex="\angle B = 180^\circ - \angle A = 180^\circ - \angle D = \angle C \quad \blacksquare">The angles at one leg add up to $180^\circ$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="isosceles-diagonals" title="The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are equal" why="Two triangles with a common base and equal legs.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD$" prove="$AC = BD$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} show="AC BD" hl="AC BD">Draw the diagonals.</Step>
<Step hl="ABD AB=DC <A=<D" tex="AB = DC,\quad AD = DA,\quad \angle A = \angle D">Look at $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle DCA$: the legs are equal, $AD$ is common, and the base angles are equal (previous property).</Step>
<Step hl="AC=BD" tex="\triangle ABD = \triangle DCA \;\Rightarrow\; BD = CA \quad \blacksquare">SAS.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="isosceles-projections" title="In an isosceles trapezoid the height divides the longer base into segments $\tfrac{a-b}{2}$ and $\tfrac{a+b}{2}$, and the diagonal is $d^2 = h^2 + \left(\tfrac{a+b}{2}\right)^2$" why="Two heights cut off two congruent right triangles, with a rectangle left in the middle.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD$, $BH$ and $CK$ the heights" prove="$AH = \tfrac{a-b}{2},\ HD = \tfrac{a+b}{2}$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} show="BH CK <BHA <CKD" hl="BH CK">Drop the heights $BH$ and $CK$.</Step>
<Step hl="AH KD" tex="AH = KD">$\triangle ABH = \triangle DCK$ (by the hypotenuse and a leg).</Step>
<Step hl="HK" tex="HK = BC = b">$HBCK$ is a rectangle.</Step>
<Step hl="AH HD" tex="AH = \frac{a - b}{2}, \qquad HD = HK + KD = \frac{a + b}{2}">$AD = AH + HK + KD = 2\,AH + b = a$.</Step>
<Step show="BD" hl="BHD" tex="d^2 = BD^2 = h^2 + \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right)^2 \quad \blacksquare">Pythagoras' theorem in the right triangle $BHD$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="isosceles-perpendicular-diagonals" title="If the diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are perpendicular, its height equals the midline: $h = \tfrac{a+b}{2}$, $S = h^2$" why="A diagonal makes $45^\circ$ with the base, so the height equals the base segment next to it.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD$, $AC \perp BD$" prove="$h = \tfrac{a+b}{2},\ S = h^2$">
<Step set={{ a: 4, b: 2, h: 3, s: 1 }} show="AC BD O" hl="<AOD">The diagonals meet at $O$ at a right angle.</Step>
<Step hl="AOD" tex="\angle OAD = \angle ODA = 45^\circ">$\triangle ABD = \triangle DCA$ (see the equal diagonals), so $\angle ODA = \angle OAD$; they add up to $90^\circ$.</Step>
<Step show="BH <BHD" hl="BHD" tex="h = BH = HD = \frac{a + b}{2}">In the right triangle $BHD$, $\angle BDH = 45^\circ$, so it is isosceles; $HD = \tfrac{a+b}{2}$ by the previous property.</Step>
<Step tex="S = \frac{a + b}{2} \cdot h = h \cdot h = h^2 \quad \blacksquare">The area formula.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="isosceles-cyclic" title="A circle can be drawn through the vertices of an isosceles trapezoid" why="The opposite angles come out at $180^\circ$.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD$" prove="$A, B, C, D$ lie on one circle">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} hl="<A <B" tex="\angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ">The angles at one leg.</Step>
<Step hl="<B=<C" tex="\angle A + \angle C = \angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ">$\angle C = \angle B$ (an isosceles trapezoid).</Step>
<Step hl="ABCD">The opposite angles add up to $180^\circ$, so a circle can be drawn through the vertices of the quadrilateral. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="criterion-base-angles" kind="converse" title="If the base angles of a trapezoid are equal, it is isosceles" why="The same two right triangles, now by a leg and an angle.">
<Proof given="$\angle A = \angle D$" prove="$AB = CD$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} show="BH CK <BHA <CKD" hl="BH=CK">Drop the heights: $BH = CK$.</Step>
<Step hl="ABH DCK <A=<D" tex="\triangle ABH = \triangle DCK">The right triangles are congruent by a leg and an acute angle.</Step>
<Step hl="AB=CD" tex="AB = DC \quad \blacksquare">The hypotenuses are equal.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="criterion-diagonals" kind="converse" title="If the diagonals of a trapezoid are equal, it is isosceles" why="Translating one diagonal forms an isosceles triangle.">
<Proof given="$AC = BD$" prove="$AB = CD$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 3, s: 1.5 }} show="AC BD CE DE E" hl="CE">Through $C$ draw a line parallel to $BD$; it meets line $AD$ at $E$. $BCED$ is a parallelogram, so $CE = BD = AC$.</Step>
<Step hl="ACE AC=CE" tex="\angle CAE = \angle CEA">$\triangle ACE$ is isosceles.</Step>
<Step hl="<CEA=<BDA" tex="\angle CAD = \angle CEA = \angle BDA">$CE \parallel BD$: corresponding angles.</Step>
<Step hl="ACD ABD" tex="\triangle ACD = \triangle DBA \;\Rightarrow\; CD = BA \quad \blacksquare">$AC = DB$, $AD$ is common, $\angle CAD = \angle BDA$: SAS.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="cyclic-is-isosceles" kind="converse" title="If a circle can be drawn through the vertices of a trapezoid, it is isosceles" why="Being cyclic and the co-interior angles together give equal base angles.">
<Proof given="$ABCD$ inscribed in a circle" prove="$AB = CD$">
<Step hl="<A <C" tex="\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ">Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral.</Step>
<Step hl="<C <D" tex="\angle C + \angle D = 180^\circ">The angles at one leg.</Step>
<Step hl="<A=<D" tex="\angle A = \angle D">Hence the base angles are equal, so (by the previous criterion) the trapezoid is isosceles. ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Properties title="Inscribed circle">

<Property id="incircle-condition" title="A circle can be inscribed in a trapezoid if and only if the sum of the bases equals the sum of the legs" why="This is the general criterion for quadrilaterals (Pitot's theorem and its converse).">
<Proof given="$ABCD$ a trapezoid, $AD = a,\ BC = b,\ AB = c,\ CD = d$" prove="has an inscribed circle $\Leftrightarrow\ a + b = c + d$">
<Step hl="AD BC">A trapezoid is a convex quadrilateral; its opposite sides are $AD, BC$ and $AB, CD$.</Step>
<Step hl="AB CD" tex="a + b = c + d">In a quadrilateral circumscribed about a circle the sums of opposite sides are equal, and conversely, when they are equal a circle can be inscribed (see the quadrilateral). ∎</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="isosceles-tangential" title="If a circle can be inscribed in an isosceles trapezoid, its leg is $\tfrac{a+b}{2}$ and its height is $h = 2r = \sqrt{ab}$" why="The previous criterion gives the leg, Pythagoras' theorem the height.">
<Proof given="$AB = CD = c$, a circle of radius $r$ inscribed in the trapezoid" prove="$c = \tfrac{a+b}{2},\ h = 2r = \sqrt{ab}$">
<Step set={{ a: 6, b: 1.5, h: 3, s: 2.25 }} show="(IT) I" hl="(IT)">The circle touches all four sides.</Step>
<Step hl="AB CD" tex="2c = a + b \;\Rightarrow\; c = \frac{a + b}{2}">By the previous property the legs add up to the sum of the bases.</Step>
<Step show="BH <BHA" hl="ABH" tex="h^2 = c^2 - \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)^2 = ab">$AH = \tfrac{a-b}{2}$ (the foot of the height), Pythagoras' theorem in $\triangle ABH$; $c = \tfrac{a+b}{2}$ and $\left(\tfrac{a+b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac{a-b}{2}\right)^2 = ab$.</Step>
<Step show="IT T" hl="IT" tex="h = 2r \quad \blacksquare">The bases are parallel tangents to the circle, so the distance between them is a diameter.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark kind="special">**Right trapezoid**: one leg is perpendicular to the bases; its height equals that leg. Try $s = 0$ on the slider to see one.</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are equal, but unlike a rectangle's they do not bisect each other: their meeting point divides them in the ratio $a : b$.</Remark>
