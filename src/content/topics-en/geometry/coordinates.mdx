<Definition>A **Cartesian coordinate system** is two perpendicular number axes with a common origin $O$: $Ox$ and $Oy$. The coordinates of a point $A(x;\, y)$ are its projections on the axes: $x$ is the **abscissa**, $y$ the **ordinate**. In the equation of a line $y = kx + b$ the number $k$ is called the **slope**.</Definition>

<Properties>

<Property id="distance-formula" title="The distance between two points is $\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$" why="The differences of the coordinates are the legs of a right triangle.">
<Proof given="$A(x_1;\, y_1)$, $B(x_2;\, y_2)$" prove="$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$">
<Step show="AC BC C <ACB" hl="ACB">$C(x_2;\, y_1)$: $AC$ is parallel to the axis $Ox$, $BC$ to $Oy$, so $\angle C = 90^\circ$.</Step>
<Step hl="AC BC" tex="AC = |x_2 - x_1|, \qquad BC = |y_2 - y_1|">The legs are parallel to the axes.</Step>
<Step hl="AB" tex="AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2} \quad \blacksquare">Pythagoras' theorem.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="midpoint-formula" title="The coordinates of the midpoint of a segment are the averages of the coordinates of its ends" why="Thales' theorem for the projections on an axis.">
<Proof given="$A(x_1;\, y_1)$, $B(x_2;\, y_2)$, $AM = MB$" prove="$M\left(\tfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2};\ \tfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$">
<Step show="AA0 BB0 MM0 A0 B0 M0 M" hl="M">Drop perpendiculars from $A$, $M$ and $B$ to the axis $Ox$: $AA_0 \parallel MM_0 \parallel BB_0$.</Step>
<Step hl="A0M0=M0B0" tex="A_0M_0 = M_0B_0">Thales' theorem: $AM = MB$.</Step>
<Step tex="x_M = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \qquad y_M = \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \quad \blacksquare">$M_0$ is the midpoint of $A_0B_0$; the same on the axis $Oy$.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="circle-equation" title="The equation of a circle with centre $(a;\, b)$ and radius $R$: $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = R^2$" why="A circle is the set of points at distance $R$ from the centre.">
<Proof given="Centre $A(a;\, b)$, radius $R$" prove="$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = R^2$">
<Step show="(AB) B" hl="(AB)">A point $(x;\, y)$ is on the circle if and only if its distance from the centre is $R$.</Step>
<Step tex="(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = R^2 \quad \blacksquare">The distance formula, squared (both sides are non-negative).</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="line-equation" title="The equation of a line is $y = kx + b$, where $k = \tfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ is the tangent of the angle of inclination" why="For all points of a line the ratio of rise to run is the same (similar triangles).">
<Proof given="The line $AB$, $x_1 \ne x_2$" prove="$y = kx + b$">
<Step show="line:AB AC BC C <ACB" hl="ACB" tex="k = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}">In $\triangle ACB$, $\tfrac{|BC|}{|AC|} = \tan\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle of the line with $Ox$; the sign shows whether the line goes up or down.</Step>
<Step show="line:AB" hl="AB" tex="\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = k \;\Rightarrow\; y = kx + (y_1 - kx_1) \quad \blacksquare">For any other point $(x;\, y)$ of the line the ratio is the same: similar right triangles.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

<Property id="perpendicular-slopes" title="The lines $y = k_1x + b_1$ and $y = k_2x + b_2$ are perpendicular if and only if $k_1k_2 = -1$" why="Pythagoras' theorem and its converse for the lines moved to the origin.">
<Proof given="$y = k_1x + b_1$, $y = k_2x + b_2$" prove="Perpendicular $\iff k_1k_2 = -1$">
<Step show="line:AB line:AD D" hl="<BAD">The slope does not change if we move the lines to the origin: $y = k_1x$ and $y = k_2x$. Take the points $P(1;\, k_1)$ and $Q(1;\, k_2)$ on them.</Step>
<Step tex="\begin{gathered}OP^2 + OQ^2 = PQ^2 \\ \iff (1 + k_1^2) + (1 + k_2^2) = (k_1 - k_2)^2\end{gathered}">The angle at $O$ is right if and only if Pythagoras' equality holds.</Step>
<Step tex="k_1 k_2 = -1 \quad \blacksquare">Expand the brackets and simplify.</Step>
</Proof>
</Property>

</Properties>

<Remark>Parallel lines have the same slope: $k_1 = k_2$ (and $b_1 \ne b_2$). A line parallel to the axis $Oy$ ($x = c$) has no slope.</Remark>

<Remark kind="mistake">In the midpoint formula the coordinates are added, in the distance formula they are subtracted. $AB = \sqrt{(x_1 + x_2)^2 + \dots}$ is a mistake.</Remark>

</Problem>
